Radiological source search requires efficient and accurate threat assessment to ensure public safety. Traditional methods rely on manual surveys with handheld detectors or vehicle-mounted systems, which can be time-consuming and expose operators to hazardous environments. Recent advances in machine learning and artificial intelligence present an opportunity to improve radiological source search capabilities. In this work, we present the integration of agentic AI and real-time radiation mapping to enhance radiological search capabilities.

We equip Boston Dynamics’ Spot quadruped robot with the LAMP-Imager, developed by Gamma Reality Inc., which comprises a radiation imager, LiDAR, IMU, camera, and an onboard computer, enabling real-time 3D radiation imaging while autonomously navigating complex environments.

We explored two approaches for autonomous navigation: reinforcement learning (RL) and agentic AI. RL-based navigation involved training Spot in a simulated environment using rewardbased learning, enabling obstacle avoidance and exploration. We also developed an agentic AI framework that integrates image captioning, semantic segmentation, and large language models (LLMs) for context-aware decision-making. This system processes camera data to recognize objects and generate scene descriptions, allowing Spot to make informed navigation decisions or communicate findings to an operator. Additionally, radiation hotspot detection tools were incorporated into the framework, enabling Spot to prioritize high-radiation areas for improved source localization. Field tests demonstrate Spot’s ability to autonomously navigate, avoid obstacles, and construct 3D radiation maps, showcasing AI’s role in intelligent, adaptive exploration.
