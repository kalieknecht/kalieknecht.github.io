Detecting and interdicting illicit radiological material remains a central challenge in nuclear security and nonproliferation. Conventional approaches such as radiation portal monitors provide high sensitivity, but impose fixed infrastructure and operational constraints, limiting their effectiveness in dynamic or distributed environments. More flexible approaches are needed to support detection and tracking in realistic operational scenarios. Reconfigurable, distributed sensor networks enable rapid deployment, adaptive placement, and coverage beyond fixed chokepoints, allowing detection and tracking in environments where traditional systems are impractical.
The Compact Radiation Detection Array for Tracking and Interdiction (CRAFTI) project has developed a reconfigurable network of portable sensor nodes that integrate radiation detectors with contextual sensing modalities, including stereo vision and LiDAR. Each node performs local data acquisition and processing, while network-level methods fuse observations across nodes to improve detection confidence and enable vehicle attribution. Machine learning techniques are applied throughout the system, including deep learning and vision-language models for vehicle re-identification, statistical and neural methods for anomaly detection, and kernel-based approaches for multimodal data fusion. These methods enable aggregation of repeated observations of a source, increasing signal-to-noise ratio and enabling detection of low-activity sources not observable in individual measurements. These approaches are demonstrated using data from a large-scale, multi-node field campaign conducted at Oak Ridge National Laboratory under realistic traffic and source conditions.
This work demonstrates how AI-enabled multimodal sensing can enhance radiological detection, tracking, and attribution, while addressing key challenges in multimodal data integration, large-scale data management, and deployment across heterogeneous sensing systems.
